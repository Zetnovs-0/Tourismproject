A Debate · Costa Blanca Two Sides

Tourism in Alicante

Two sides
of tourism

The same visitors who fill the cafés and fund the museums also fill the streets in August and price locals out of the centre. A look at what tourism gives — and what it takes.

For Culture, trade, income.
Against Crowding, rents, saturation.
Alicante · Tourism Debate 01 — Context
01

A record-breaking city — and a one-industry economy

+ For
  • 1.5 million tourists in 2025 — a historic record.
  • Foreign visitors +20%, reaching 935,000.
  • €11.56 billion in tourism spending, up 9.9%.
− Against
  • “Highly dependent on tourism” — no second engine if demand falls.
  • Summer population +12.39% — above 407,000.
  • Airport traffic doubled since 2004: infrastructure chasing arrivals.
Alicante · Tourism Debate 02 — Economy & Employment
02

Whose growth? Jobs versus job quality

+ For
  • €1,360 per trip, €137.50 per day — money into local businesses.
  • €84 million in cruise impact forecast for 2026.
  • Called an “indispensable motor of employment and wealth”.
− Against
  • Unemployment spikes every winter as seasonal contracts end.
  • Youth unemployment stays among the EU’s highest — tourism hasn’t absorbed the workforce.
  • Part-time and underemployment mask the real picture.
Alicante · Tourism Debate 03 — Housing & Cost of Living
03

A young worker earns €1,100 — and pays €900 for a one-bed flat

Take-home, 18–24 €1,100
One-bed rent €900
+ For
  • Rental income funds renovation of ageing stock.
  • Investment regenerates streets and businesses in the old town.
  • Small owners gain income from family property.
− Against
  • Rent eats 82% of salary. The recommended ceiling is 30%.
  • Tourist flats: 3,134 → 4,320 between 2021 and 2024.
  • Young people and families pushed out of the centre entirely.
Alicante · Tourism Debate 04 — Cruise Tourism
04

A twelve-fold boom — value or just volume?

+ For
  • 18,000 → 230,000 passengers between 2017 and 2025.
  • 325,000 forecast for 2026 — a record.
  • Home-port status means passengers spend before and after sailing.
− Against
  • Food and excursions are prepaid on board — little lands locally.
  • 325,000 more arrivals pile onto a saturated peak season.
  • Volume is not value.
Alicante · Tourism Debate 05 — Services & Seasonality
05

Who pays for the infrastructure? Funding versus overload

+ For
  • Tourism revenue feeds the municipal budget for cleaning, security and transport.
  • 80% hotel occupancy in February 2026 — seasonality is easing.
  • Year-round visitors support year-round businesses.
− Against
  • “Strong pressure” on public services — UA Vectalia Mobility report.
  • Strain on transport, waste, health centres and schools.
  • Growth without investment is a deferred crisis.
Alicante · Tourism Debate 06 — Vulnerability
06

Exposed to shocks it cannot control

+ For
  • Hotel activity surpassed 2019 by 2024.
  • Regional conflicts redirect demand toward Alicante.
  • Fuel prices moderated in 2024, easing travel costs.
− Against
  • COVID cut tourism revenue 64.7% in 2020 — one pandemic erased years of growth.
  • Conflicts push fuel prices up — a regional conflict can cause a record energy shock.
  • No buffer: housing already absorbs most of a young salary.
